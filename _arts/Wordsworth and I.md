𝗪𝗶𝗹𝗹𝗶𝗮𝗺 𝗪𝗼𝗿𝗱𝘀𝘄𝗼𝗿𝘁𝗵 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗜

During my undergrad studies, I took an elective course in World Literature. In the first lecture, our professor asked each of us to imagine a moment of deep happiness and express what we felt.

I immediately improvised two lines of poetry (in Arabic):

رأيتُ الكونَ مِن حولي .. يفيضُ سعادةً ورضا
كأنَّ اللهَ لا يقضي .. سوى ما أرتضيهِ قضا

Which may be translated as:

“𝘐 𝘴𝘢𝘸 𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘶𝘯𝘪𝘷𝘦𝘳𝘴𝘦 𝘢𝘳𝘰𝘶𝘯𝘥 𝘮𝘦
𝘖𝘷𝘦𝘳𝘧𝘭𝘰𝘸 𝘸𝘪𝘵𝘩 𝘫𝘰𝘺 𝘢𝘯𝘥 𝘱𝘦𝘢𝘤𝘦;
𝘈𝘴 if 𝘎𝘰𝘥 𝘥𝘦𝘤𝘳𝘦𝘦𝘴 𝘯𝘰𝘵𝘩𝘪𝘯𝘨
𝘌𝘹𝘤𝘦𝘱𝘵 𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘧𝘢𝘵𝘦 𝘵𝘩𝘢𝘵 𝘐 𝘸𝘰𝘶𝘭𝘥 𝘱𝘭𝘦𝘢𝘴𝘦!”
(Trans. GPT-5.1)

Upon hearing the lines, the professor commented:

“This is very similar to what William Wordsworth does in his Rainbow poem, which begins:
‘𝘔𝘺 𝘩𝘦𝘢𝘳𝘵 𝘭𝘦𝘢𝘱𝘴 𝘶𝘱 𝘸𝘩𝘦𝘯 𝘐 𝘣𝘦𝘩𝘰𝘭𝘥
𝘈 𝘳𝘢𝘪𝘯𝘣𝘰𝘸 𝘪𝘯 𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘴𝘬𝘺’

Yet, the difference is that Wordsworth saw the beauty of the universe reflected within himself, while AbdulRahman saw his own happiness reflected in the entire universe!”

I had never encountered that poem before, but that was one of the highlights of my undergraduate years. It also showed me how people can express their feelings in similar ways across different languages and cultures, a passion that is now reflected in my NLP research interests.
